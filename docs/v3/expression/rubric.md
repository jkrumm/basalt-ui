# Expression rubric

For the reviewer only. Score each item 0 to 3 from the shots and the scenario source, and cite a shot or a `file:line` for every score under 3.

| Score | Meaning                                      |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | Absent, or wrong                             |
| 1     | Attempted; a viewer still has to work for it |
| 2     | Works; a designer would change one thing     |
| 3     | Nothing to change                            |

`n/a` is allowed where the case has no such thing (no baseline in the data, nothing live). An `n/a` item leaves the sum and the maximum. F3 is `n/a` where the data holds no reference to stand a value against: one period, no target, no best, no budget, no prior period and no peer to compare with. A part-to-whole's whole is not a baseline, so a share of one year's total is no reference, and the missing target, prior year or delta pill costs nothing (cycle 8: n32's reviewer ruled F3 `n/a` on data with no target, best or prior period, as cycle 7's n24 was on one 30-day window; n29's reviewer scored F3 2 on one tax year for 'no target, prior year or delta pill', expression-8.26). Where the data does hold a reference, F3 scores whether the value stands against it.

Three items no still frame shows are read from what the shoot records. K3 scores the hosts the keyboard log shows: a host the log does not reach is not marked down, because the log covers one host by design (cycle 8: n29's reviewer docked a point because a second host was 'not shown to be reachable', expression-8.32). A keyboard frame whose focused element is a scroll region (a tabbable box that only scrolls, such as `div[role=region]`) is no evidence of a host's keys, since the arrow keys scroll it and announce nothing: read such a log as no evidence, never as a missing announcement, and take the host's keys from `basalt-ui shot --states keys` on the same build (cycle 8: n32's round-0 reviewer scored K3 1 from a scroll-region frame, where the shipped tool on the same build walked the table rows and announced each Issue, expression-8.58 and .62). The keyboard frame presses the arrow keys the focused host's role takes (ArrowRight for a chart group, ArrowDown for a grid or table row). With one tab stop the focused element is the same before and after the keys, so a log that shows it unchanged is not a defect; the announcement the keyboard log records after each key (which item, in reading order) is the move. Where the keys move DOM focus between rows, the move is read from the focused element, and an aria-live announcement is not required. X3: the list frame, the pointer on the second row of a list that shares the page's focus, with the dim state of the part it joins; X3 scores both directions from that frame and the keyboard frame. A part is a chart, a list, a table or a timeline. A strip of stat cells that counts a list's rows is no part: it shows counts and no items. Two parts are linked parts when they show the same items, or one item and its children, with or without a chart: a probes table beside its findings table is scored, and so is a list whose rows are a chart's series or items the chart could draw (the cost of not drawing them is F1's or X2's). X3 is `n/a` when the page has one part (a strip of cells over one table is one part), and when it has two or more and no two of them show the same items or an item and its children: a month chart beside a ridge-by-azimuth chart share no items, so there is nothing to link, and a link the question needed that the page lacks is F1's or X2's, never X3's (cycle 14: n68's reviewer scored X3 2 on exactly that and asked the lead to rule between `n/a` and 2, while n66's scored the same structure 3 as 'another grain, so not joining them is correct', expression-14.42; the first reading holds). Cycle 9 read this two ways: n33's reviewers scored X3 on two tables, a probe and its findings, with no chart on the page, and neither reading of the old sentence settled it (a probe and its findings shared no focus, so it scored 1, expression-9.11); n36's round-0 reviewer scored X3 1 on two stat cells over one table and said in its report that the lead might rule a strip no chart part, and its round-1 reviewer ruled `n/a` on the same structure ('there is no chart and the stat cards show counts, not items', expression-9.40). The second reading holds for n36: the strip is no part, the page has one, and X3 is `n/a`. X4: the pointer sweep, a frame with the pointer at the gap between two adjacent marks and the dim state that frame showed. Where nothing on the page dims the rest when the pointer or the keys focus an item (a table row that only tints is no dimming), X4 is `n/a` and the missing response is F7's: a cap on X4 would charge the same defect twice. Where marks dim but the cycle has no sweep, X4 scores the dimmed rest alone, the score line says "no sweep", and X4 is at most 2. Cycle 9 read the first case two ways with the same evidence, no sweep and nothing that dims: n33's round-0 reviewer ruled X4 `n/a` ('no marks dim; the missing response is F7's', expression-9.5) and n36's scored 2 ('nothing dims, which caps it at 2', expression-9.41). `n/a` holds; the cap of 2 is for a page where something dims, has adjacent marks to sweep (bars, cells, rows, spans on one row) and no sweep was shot. Cycle 13 read it on pages of line charts two ways with one result: n58's and n59's reviewers capped X4 at 2 because the SWEEP line found no two adjacent marks ('no sweep: no adjacent marks on line charts ... capped at 2', expression-13.22 and .31), a frame no builder can shoot across line series, so no page of lines could score 3. Where every chart on the page is a line, or the SWEEP line says no two adjacent marks were found, the missing sweep is the shoot's and not the page's: X4 scores the dim state the list frame and the keyboard frame show (a joined table's rows, a band), 3 is reachable, the score line says 'no adjacent marks', and no cap applies. Cycle 10 read a line chart's x focus two ways (n39 charged X4 2 for lines that do not dim, n42 did not): the lines owe no dim at an x focus, so X4 scores what else dims and is `n/a` where nothing does (the F7 row gives the reading).

## Rendering (the owner sets the bar, review 2)

Added on 2026-11-18 from the owner's first hands-on review of the playground (`docs/v3/expression/calibration-owner-2.md`). F1 scores whether the idea fits; these score how it is drawn and how it holds still, so a good idea drawn badly cannot pass. A defect is charged once: a hover jump is R3's and not K6's, a flash at a gap X4's, a clipped value F6's; a clipped, cut or ellipsised title or label is R2's, whoever drew it. The same holds across every item, F, K, X and R alike: the item whose row names a defect owns it, and a second item charges only what it loses beyond that, never the same frame and the same loss again. R2 charges a rendering defect another item already charged only where it adds a rendering fault of its own, and K5 charges a 390 frame only for what is lost beyond the label F6 charged (cycle 15: n69's K5 for the heat grid with no values, expression-15.4). A misplaced, lone or colliding data label is F6's and a card-height mismatch is X2's, so R2 does not charge them again (cycle 14: n64's reviewer charged R2 the '92' total and the 611 and 635 px card heights, already F6 and X2, expression-14.19, and n68's the lone '85/129' label, already F6, expression-14.43; only n64's uneven heatmap ticks were R2's own).

| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R1  | **Separator weight.** A rule between rows, list entries or cards is at most the grid token: as faint as the chart's grid lines, in both schemes. A rule that reads as ink scores 1 at most (a bare `border`, `border-b` or `divide-y` paints in the text colour, `border-line` is the data-line grey), and ink rules on every row of a list score 0. Look at the 1800 rest frame, light and dark, and set each row rule against the plot's grid lines. `n/a` when nothing separates items with a rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| R2  | **Craft of drawn marks.** Hand-composed marks and the parts around them are drawn with care, scored apart from the idea: rows, tracks and their labels start on one shared x and one rhythm; every label and title fits its box at 1800 and 390 (none clipped, cut at a card or panel edge, hidden under a mark or ellipsised, a title as much as a value); marks of one class have one size (one bar thickness, one dot radius, one row height). Look at the rest and fixture frames at both viewports. "A designer would redraw it" is 1, "a viewer has to decode it" is 0. A clipped, cut or ellipsised label or title counts the same whether the app or a kit part drew it: the viewer cannot read it either way, so a page of shipped parts earns no default 3, and a track or chain with ellipsised titles, or cut at its panel edge, scores 1 at most (3 or more such cuts on one page, or a title lost at 390, is 0 or 1 by the count the shots show) |
| R3  | **Still under hover.** Pointing at or stepping to a mark changes colour and the readout only. Set the `--focus--` and `--sweep-gap--` frames against the rest frame at the same viewport and scheme: the plot's top-left corner, its axes and the edges of the parts beside it sit at the same pixel position. A shift of a few pixels, a readout or tooltip that grows the card, or a label that wraps on focus scores 1 at most; a shift of a row or more, or a plot that changes size, scores 0. A jump that loops (a hover line pushes the plot down, the pointer leaves it, the line collapses) can fall between two still frames, so where the shoot logs the plot box along the pointer sweep, a move in the log counts as a shift in a frame. `n/a` only when neither frame exists                                                                                                                                                                     |
| R4  | **Records view.** A grid of five or more cards that each show one record of one shape (one per measure, Issue or host) offers a table view of the same records, through a toggle in the header or a table beside the grid, or is a table. A grid of records with no table view scores 1 at most. `n/a` when the page has no such grid                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

**The rendering cap.** F4, R1, R2 and R3 are cap items: a 0 or a 1 on any one of them caps the case's score at 79, under the pass line of 80, whatever the sum. The score line names the cap and gives the uncapped score beside it, `79 (capped by R2 1; uncapped 84)`, so the trend still reads.

## Score and verdict

`score = 100 × sum / (3 × items scored)`, rounded, then the rendering cap above.

Severity of a lost point, for the findings table: a 0 on F1, F2 or any K item is P1. Any other 0 or 1 is P2. A 2 is P3. An X or R item counts like an F item.

The X items apply from cycle 3, and the R items and the rendering cap from cycle 11. For the trend against cycles 1 and 2, the cycle file also reports each score on the F and K items alone, and from cycle 11 also the score without the R items and the cap.

For a referenced case, also state which of the two answers the case's question faster for a first-time viewer, in one sentence with the reason: `build`, `tie` or `reference`. Judge the question, not the styling.

Report per case: the item scores with citations, the score, the verdict, and the devices from this list the build reached: metaphor, hero number, baseline delta, colour budget, quantized marks, threshold geometry, pre-annotation, direct labels, live cue, dim-the-rest, keyboard parity, anatomy.
