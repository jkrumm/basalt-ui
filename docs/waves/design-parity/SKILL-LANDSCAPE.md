## Bottom line

- **Anthropic ships two unrelated things.** `frontend-design` is open in `anthropics/skills` and teaches a two-pass "plan then build" with a _soft, unbounded_ screenshot self-critique and **zero chart guidance**; `/dataviz` is a bundled Claude Code skill (first shipped in v2.1.198, 1 July 2026) whose real transferable assets are a **computable palette validator**, an "is it even a chart?" form table, and a **stat-tile / meter / hero-figure contract** — the closest published match anywhere to basalt's KPI grammar.
- **`pbakaus/impeccable`'s** transferable ideas are its 59-rule deterministic edit-hook detector, its two isolated critique subagents with a `DEGRADED` banner, and its scored Nielsen `/40` rubric — but its craft floor explicitly **bans the hero-metric tile, progress rings and identical/nested cards**, i.e. Keel Workspace's KPI vocabulary.
- **`ui-ux-pro-max`** is a ~3MB searchable CSV corpus behind a 16KB SKILL.md; its rule _rows_ are genuinely useful for dense UI (density dial, bullet-chart grids, tabular numerals, motion caps), but it ranks **"Charts & Data" #10 of 10 at "LOW" impact**, and the only independent head-to-head test calls its output "the most generic page".
- **The Cursor/AGENTS.md ecosystem is structurally weak**: `.mdc` frontmatter triples, no arbitration when rules merge, and no pack ships a design lint, CI gate or screenshot eval; the controlled studies of repository context files are null-to-negative on task success while costing more.
- **Vercel's `vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines`** is a behaviour/a11y/perf checklist (MIT, three prose artifacts, **no linter, no rule IDs, no severity scale**) with exactly one chart sentence and no table guidance — a complement to basalt, not a competitor.
- **shadcn has the most complete agent surface** (MCP inside the CLI, registry JSON, an official SKILL.md whose dashboard recipe is `Sidebar + Card + Chart + Table`); **Tremor Raw is the live track** and its 29 KPI blocks are the richest published stat-tile vocabulary, while `@tremor/react` is frozen; none of it installs into Mantine.
- **No design skill anywhere has a published evaluation.** Refactoring UI has **no dashboard chapter and no table chapter**, so every "Refactoring-UI-derived" dashboard rule in the wild is extrapolation; the nearest yardsticks are Design2Code fidelity, WebDev Arena preference and VisualWebArena task success.

## Anthropic: `frontend-design` (open) and `/dataviz` (bundled)

**`frontend-design`** lives at `skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` in `anthropics/skills` (181,674 stars / 21,522 forks, created 2025-09-22, last push 2026-10-09). Frontmatter is **three fields only** — `name`, `description`, `license` — with no `allowed-tools`; the repo README confirms the base contract "requires only two fields": name and description. Its process is: fix the subject/audience/job → build a compact token system (colour as "4–6 named hex values", layout as "one-sentence prose descriptions and ASCII wireframes") → "review that plan against the brief before building" → build → self-critique. The screenshot loop is **real but soft**: "Critique your own work as you build, taking screenshots to review if your environment supports it — a picture is worth 1000 tokens." No round count, no reviewer subagent, no browser harness, no bundled scripts or references. Its most reusable section is five named AI-design "tells", including the warm cream/serif/terracotta palette near `#D97757` ("Anthropic's own Claude-interaction accent") and "the SaaS-card kit: content chopped into identical rounded cards, one border-radius on everything regardless of hierarchy". It carries **no chart, axis, palette-by-role, tooltip, number-formatting or KPI guidance**; its only data-adjacent line warns that "a big number with a small label, supporting stats, and a gradient accent is the default treatment, so only use it if that's truly the best option."

**`/dataviz` is not in that repo** — the `skills/` tree holds 19 folders and no `dataviz`. It ships with the closed-source CLI and is documented as a bundled Skill: it "picks the chart form for the data, assigns color by role, validates the palette for colorblind safety and contrast with a bundled script, and applies mark, interaction, and accessibility rules. Uses a brand-neutral placeholder palette that you replace with your own." The changelog pins its arrival to **`## 2.1.198`** ("Added `/dataviz` skill for chart and dashboard design guidance with a runnable color-palette validator"), and the version has been corrected three times since — 2.1.210 (OKLab perceptual difference, "recalibrated color-blindness thresholds"), 2.1.216 (palette reordered; "fixed guidance that suggested direct labels for four-series charts") and 2.1.296 (lighter violet for series 7; "compact y-axis labels like 1K"). That churn is itself a finding: the palette rules were wrong on first ship.

The body is only available via a third-party mirror of the CLI, so treat the following as **version-pinned, not Anthropic-vouched**. Structurally SKILL.md is a **router**: a 7-step procedure, non-negotiables, a design-system parameter table, and an index into seven references plus `scripts/validate_palette.js` (a `.py` twin also ships). The thesis is the single most transferable line in this entire landscape: _"the color part is computable, so compute it. Never eyeball whether a palette is colorblind-safe — run `scripts/validate_palette.js`."_ And "Color comes LAST. Most bad charts pick colors first."

Non-negotiables worth lifting verbatim: "One axis. Never a dual-axis chart" (called "the #1 chart mistake"); "Assign categorical hues in fixed order, never cycled. A 9th series is never a generated hue — it folds into 'Other'"; "Color follows the entity, never its rank"; "Sequential = one hue, light→dark. Diverging = two hues + a neutral gray midpoint. Never a rainbow"; "Text wears text tokens, never the series color"; status colours are reserved and "ship with an icon + label, never color alone". The validator's six checks: fixed hue order; lightness band OKLCH L ≈ 0.43–0.77 light / 0.48–0.67 dark; chroma floor C ≥ ~0.10; CVD ΔE ≥ 8 target, 6–8 floor only with secondary encoding; **normal-vision worst-pair ΔE ≥ 15 as a hard gate that secondary encoding does not excuse**; ≥ 3:1 contrast for marks; documented palette only.

Chart selection is an "is it even a chart?" table: a single current value → **stat tile** (not a one-bar bar chart); a handful of headline numbers → **KPI row** (not a grouped bar chart); the number a dashboard leads with → **hero figure** (≥48px, sans, exactly one per view); a single ratio against a limit → **meter** with a same-ramp track (not a pie of two slices); more than ~7 meaningful classes → a table, not more colours. The series ladder is explicit: 1–3 series "color alone is comfortable"; at 4, "direct labels become mandatory" and all-pairs forms (scatter, bubble, choropleth, small multiples) cap at **three**; 7–8 is the token ceiling.

The KPI contract is the most directly useful artifact in this report: **stat tile** = `label` (sentence case, no trailing colon) · `value` (sans semibold, auto-compacted as `1,284 / 12.9K / $4.2M`) · optional `delta` (signed, against a named period, colour = direction × whether up is good) · optional `trend` (12-point sparkline in the de-emphasis hue, current period in the accent). **Meter**: fill carries severity, "the unfilled track is a lighter step of the same ramp". Marks: bars ≤ 24px thick with a 4px rounded data-end square at the baseline; lines 2px; markers ≥ 8px; area fills ~10% opacity; gridlines hairline, solid, recessive. Number typography: "Y-axis ticks: round to clean numbers, compact from 1,000 up (0 / 1K / 2K)"; **"Proportional figures for big numbers; tabular only in columns"** — because `tabular-nums` gives every digit a `0`'s width, so `121` looks loose at display size. Legends: always present for ≥2 series, never for one, "never a number on every point", "Direct labels before gridlines; gridlines before a second axis". Tooltips: mandatory hover layer by default ("Omitting it is the exception (a bare stat tile)"), "**Tooltips enhance, they never gate**", crosshair finds X on lines, the mark is the hit target on bars, one tooltip for every series, "Values lead, labels follow", ≥24px transparent hit area. Dashboard filters: one left-aligned row above the charts, date range first, filters scope everything below, and "**Refetch keeps the frame** — charts hold their previous render at reduced opacity, no skeleton, no layout jump".

Output stack: **no React, Tailwind or shadcn anywhere**. Components are "assembled in plain HTML/SVG", palette slots are "CSS custom properties in a local `<style>` block", dark mode is native `prefers-color-scheme` plus a `data-theme` scope, filters are "ordinary HTML form controls". The _rules_ port to Mantine; the _build instructions_ do not.

## `pbakaus/impeccable`

Not one artifact but five: a Claude Code skill (24 commands routed through `skill/reference/*.md`), an npm shim (`impeccable@4.6.0`, zero dependencies, node ≥18.19.0, published 2026-10-15), a plugin marketplace, a Rust engine binary that auto-downloads on first run, and VS Code/Chrome extensions. Repo: Apache-2.0, created 2025-11-16, last push 2026-10-16, **82,160 stars / 4,933 forks / 46 open issues**, latest tag `cli-v4.6.0`. Self-description: "1 skill, 24 commands, live browser iteration, and 59 deterministic detector rules".

Process. `/impeccable init` writes `PRODUCT.md`; `new-work` asks 2–3 questions, derives seven visual systems, then a **`concept-seed` roll** assigns a direction presented on a served decision page with re-roll registers; "writing artifact code before concept-seed has run and its assignment acknowledged is a contract violation". Before any edit the agent must load `craft-floor.md`. Verification is one **batched look** at the render, not a screenshot per tweak. `critique` **mandates two isolated parallel sub-agents** (A design review, B detector + browser evidence) that must not see each other's output; inline execution requires a `⚠️ DEGRADED: single-context (<reason>)` banner as the report's first line. Rubric: Nielsen's 10 heuristics scored 0–4 with a total out of 40 (renormalised, e.g. `/32`, when heuristics are n/a), P0–P3 severity with a fix and a suggested command each, persona red flags, persistence to `.impeccable/critique/` with a trend line over the last 5 runs, and a mandatory closing question set — skippable only via a literal `Questions skipped: <reason>` line.

The measurable claim that matters most for basalt: CLAUDE.md reports blind ratings where "comps of working screens and documents scored 2.0 to 2.9 across five rule changes while code-led builds of the same briefs scored 3 to 3.5 and were always complete."

Density heuristics are good and specific: Operate mode grants "Density. Tables with many rows, panels with many labels"; one type family; **fixed** rem scale, not fluid; a tight 1.125–1.2 step ratio; "Data and compact UI can run denser; tables at 120ch+ are fine"; a Restrained colour posture with a second neutral layer for sidebars/toolbars; a full state vocabulary; 150–250ms transitions; skeletons not in-content spinners; overlays must escape overflow ancestors. Cognitive-load caps are hard-coded: ≤4 items per group, ≤4 visible options at a decision point, ≤5 top-level nav items, ≤4 sibling choices per sidebar level. Accessibility is deliberately **quarantined into `audit`** because "models over-cautious themselves into safe, underdesigned output when reminded about accessibility at design time."

The conflict with basalt is direct and worth stating plainly. The craft floor demotes exactly Keel's vocabulary: "The hero-metric template: big number, small label, supporting stats, accent"; "Sparklines, progress rings, and soft-shadowed rounded rectangles standing in for content"; "Same-size cards of icon plus heading plus text as the page structure. Cards are the lazy container; nested cards are always wrong." A dashboard built to basalt's reference design would trip three of these. The honest reading is that these bans target marketing and AI-slop surfaces, and the underlying _test_ ("is this micro-visual carrying a job or standing in for content?") is exactly right — the ban is over-broad for operational UI.

Adoption/critique: a third-party blog (2026-07-25) put it at "49.7k GitHub stars, 266k npm installs in the past month" and reported that its Hacker News debut "went badly" with commenters unconvinced by before/after examples; a review scored it 8.4/10; the 59-vs-61 rule count is disputed between the vendor and a third-party page. Star figures are timestamps, not properties: 49.7k in July, 82,160 in October.

## `ui-ux-pro-max`

Canonical: `nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill`, MIT, created 2025-11-30, **136,705 stars / 14,530 forks**, last push 2026-10-15, release **v2.15.0 (2026-08-13)** version-synced to npm `ui-ux-pro-max-cli@2.15.0`. Three install paths coexist (plugin marketplace, `npm install -g ui-ux-pro-max-cli` then `uipro init --ai claude`, and a `npx skills add …` form the repo itself never prints).

It is a **queryable local database, not a prompt**: ~3MB of CSV ("79 searchable styles (50 active), 192 product palettes …, 74 font pairings, 119 UX guidelines, 105 icons, 17 GSAP presets, 25 chart types, and 22 stacks") behind a Python-3-stdlib BM25 `scripts/search.py`, with only the ~16KB SKILL.md on the load path and `references/quick-reference.md` / `pro-rules.md` read on demand. The query contract is disciplined: `--design-system` for new pages, one `--domain` for a targeted concern, 2–5 terms with one dominant intent, **retry once**, and "Never present a 0-result search as if it returned data".

Its dense-UI rules are genuinely good and very citable: a `--density` dial where high = "Dense/dashboard (8-32px spacing scale)", unset meaning unchanged; `number-tabular` for "data columns, prices, and timers to prevent layout shift"; `gridline-subtle` grey-200; `trend-emphasis` ("avoid heavy gradients/shadows that obscure the data"); `line-length-control` "desktop 60–75 chars"; a fixed type scale `12 14 16 18 24 32`; motion caps ("Animate 1-2 key elements per view max", exit ≈60–70% of enter, stagger 30–50ms, transform/opacity only); form rules (visible labels, error below field with `aria-describedby`, validate on blur). Chart rows are the most concrete part-to-whole guidance found anywhere: gauge for "Performance vs Target" with "Single metric per gauge; for 3+ KPIs use bullet chart grid layout", **bullet chart "Ideal for 3–10 bullet charts in a grid"**, line rejected for "Fewer than 4 data points (use stat card)", bar → ">50: paginated table", streaming only "≥1 Hz".

The fatal flaw for this use case: its own priority table ranks the ten categories and places **"Charts & Data" at priority 10 with impact "LOW"** — below accessibility, touch, performance, style and layout. Combine that with `data-density`'s "Limit information density per chart to avoid cognitive overload; split into multiple charts if needed" and you get a skill whose advice actively opposes the composite multi-metric card. The design-system output template is landing-page shaped (Hero / Services / Testimonials / Booking / Contact), which is the mechanism behind Paralect's verdict: "Clean and accessible, but **the most generic page**… Skip it if standing out is the goal: matching your product to a category gives you that category's typical look." Skillselion is sharper: "Skip it if what you want is taste. It hands you a matched row and a set of anti-patterns, and it has no argument about why a page should look one way rather than another." Also reported: manifest rot (`skill.json` / `plugin.json` say "84 UI styles… 98 UX guidelines" against 79/119 shipped); every SKILL.md command is anchored at `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}` so copy-style installs break; `pro-rules.md` is scoped to native/mobile yet sits on the pre-delivery path. The name does **not** uniquely identify it: ClaudeKit ships a different `ck:ui-ux-pro-max` with different counts, and several forks/reposts circulate.

## Cursor rules, `.mdc` and AGENTS.md

**AGENTS.md** is plain Markdown with no required fields, "used by over 60k open-source projects", credited to Codex/Amp/Jules/Cursor/Factory and stewarded by the Agentic AI Foundation. Nesting is the scaling mechanism: "the closest one takes precedence", with 88 files in the main OpenAI repo, and "explicit user chat prompts override everything".

**Cursor's own format** is the structural constraint everyone else works around: project rules must be `.mdc`; "A plain .md file in `.cursor/rules` is ignored by the rules system because it has no frontmatter to specify description, globs, and alwaysApply." The triple determines inclusion (always-on / auto-attached on glob / agent-decided / `@rule`-only), and — decisively — "Rules are applied in this order: Team Rules → Project Rules → User Rules. **All applicable rules are merged**; earlier sources take precedence when guidance conflicts." Cursor's own advice is a warning about the whole genre: "Copying entire style guides: Use a linter instead."

**What's actually in the field is thin.** `PatrickJS/awesome-cursorrules` (40,938 stars / 3,509 forks, CC0-1.0, last push 2026-05-30) is flat monolithic `rules/*.mdc`. Its best design file (`toss-style-design-system.mdc`) is genuinely decent — grayscale hierarchy, one accent, "Never use pure black text", "Avoid nested cards and excessive borders", "status is never communicated by color alone", WCAG AA — and says **nothing** about tables, charts, density, number formatting or states. Others are hollow: `cursor-ai-react-typescript-shadcn-ui-…mdc` contains the heading "UI and Styling" with _no content beneath it_, and `react-nextjs-ui-development-…mdc` is ~20 lines of "think step-by-step" with `globs: **/*` and zero UI heuristics. cursor.directory's `Uiux Design` plugin ships "1 rule" — a single prompt blob covering eleven topics, with WCAG and inline validation but no dashboard, table, chart or empty-state rule and no copy count shown.

**The one well-architected pack is also the least adopted:** `spencergoldade/cursor-designer` (35 stars, GPL-3.0, v1.0.0 2026-03-02) with always-on `core/*`, glob-scoped `frontend/*` (including a real `ui-layout-and-density.mdc` with "Too tight: cramped UIs that make scanning and tapping difficult" / "Too loose: excessive whitespace that forces unnecessary scrolling"), and agent-requested `binders/*` plus `design/*` doc templates — genuine progressive disclosure. It also names the failure modes candidly: "When guidance overlaps, the model sees both" and "stale rules are worse than none." It ships no linter, CI or test.

## Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines

Canonical slug is **`vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines`** — `vercel/…` 404s and there is no npm package of that name. MIT, created 2025-09-10, last push 2026-10-05, **no releases**, newest commit `434b7f9` "Merge pull request #29 … desktop-overscroll-guideline" (Oct 5, 2026).

One ruleset, three artifacts, **none machine-checkable**: `README.md` (prose, = what `vercel.com/design/guidelines` renders), `AGENTS.md` (MUST/SHOULD/NEVER — the only severity vocabulary in the project), `command.md` (the review prompt, regrouped under 17 headings with an `Output Format` block). No `package.json`, no `llms.txt` (both 404), no linter, no ESLint plugin, no test harness, **no rule IDs**. Distribution is a separate repo's skill (`web-design-guidelines`, `version: "1.0.0"`, author `vercel`) that **re-fetches** `command.md` before every review and emits terse `file:line` findings ("src/Button.tsx:42 - icon button missing aria-label … No preamble"), plus a `curl … | bash` installer that writes into Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode, Amp, Windsurf, Antigravity and Gemini CLI. Verification is an LLM reading prose; DeepWiki corroborates "proactive generation standards in `AGENTS.md` and reactive auditing via the `/web-interface-guidelines` command." Vercel's own docs count it as "100+ rules covering accessibility, performance, and UX".

Coverage for data-dense UI is thin and specific: **tabular figures** ("Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` or a monospace like Geist Mono") is the _only_ number-alignment rule — nothing on right-aligning numeric columns, column widths, sortable-header announcement, sticky headers, row density or zebra treatment. Tables get one line ("prefer native `button`, `a`, `label`, `table` before ARIA") with no `<th scope>`, caption or keyboard grid navigation. Number/date formatting is well handled (`Intl.DateTimeFormat` / `Intl.NumberFormat`, plus Vercel-specific "display currency with either 0 or 2 decimal places, never mix both" and "Use numerals for counts"). Loading: "Skeletons mirror final content exactly", a ~150–300ms show-delay with ~300–500ms minimum visible time, and "Show a loading indicator & keep the original label" on buttons. Empty states: "All states designed. Empty, sparse, dense, & error states" plus "No dead ends." Focus: `:focus-visible` over `:focus`, `:focus-within` for compound controls, "Sticky headers, footers, banners, & overlays never cover the focused element", `NEVER: outline: none without visible focus replacement`. Tooltips get exactly two rules ("Inline help first… tooltips as a last resort"; "Delay the first tooltip in a group") and there is **no popover/menu guidance at all** — a real gap for dense dashboards. Charts get **one sentence**: "Accessible charts. Use color-blind-friendly palettes."

Two structural caveats for a Mantine app. First, the vocabulary is Next/Tailwind even while claiming agnosticism (`focus-visible:ring-*`, `min-w-0`, `line-clamp-*`, `priority`, `suppressHydrationWarning`, React Scan) — no rule is RSC-specific, so nothing breaks, but the Hydration section is moot for pure CSR and the class-level directives need translation into Mantine theme tokens. Second, there is an **internal contradiction**: the README offers `maximum-scale=1` as a mobile fix while `command.md` lists `maximum-scale=1` as an anti-pattern and `AGENTS.md` says "NEVER: Disable browser zoom". An independent reviewer positions the artifact correctly: "not a generator but a quality gate… Audits UI code… fetching the latest rules at review time so they stay current. Pairs perfectly with a generation skill", and advises that for "a data-dense dashboard/admin rather than a marketing-forward store, de-emphasize … boldness and lean on Vercel guidelines + a design-system skill."

## Component-library agent surfaces: shadcn, Tremor, 21st.dev

**shadcn/ui** has the most complete surface of any vendor here. `shadcn mcp` ships _inside_ the CLI (`@modelcontextprotocol/sdk ^1.32.0` is a direct dependency of `shadcn@4.22.0`, published 2026-10-16); one command writes client config, and the server browses/searches/installs across **any** shadcn-compatible registry including namespaced private ones (`headers: { Authorization: "Bearer ${REGISTRY_TOKEN}" }`). What the agent gets back is registry JSON, not a bundle: `registry-item.json` with `type`, `files[]` + `target` placeholders (`@ui/`, `@lib/`) that resolve through the user's `components.json` aliases, `registryDependencies` (bare / `@ns/item` / `owner/repo/item#tag` / URL / local path), `cssVars`, `css` (with `tailwind` explicitly DEPRECATED in favour of `cssVars.theme`). The in-repo `skills/shadcn/SKILL.md` is a real style guide: `shadcn info --json` injects project context, and the principles are "Use existing components first", "Compose, don't reinvent" — _**Dashboard = Sidebar + Card + Chart + Table**_ — "Use built-in variants", "Use semantic colors", with a workflow that mandates `shadcn docs <component>`, `--dry-run`/`--diff`, and **reading and verifying every file a community registry adds**.

**Tremor** has two live tracks and one frozen one. `tremor.so` is **Tremor Raw** (copy-paste, "an adaptation of Radix Primitives styled with Tailwind", credits shadcn/ui, no Figma file); `blocks.tremor.so` is 300+ blocks, freed under MIT after **Vercel acquired Tremor on 22 Jan 2025** (announcement cites "over 16,000 stars, 300,000 monthly downloads, and 5,500,000 installs to date", 35 components, 300 blocks). The npm library `@tremor/react` is **frozen but not deprecated**: 3.18.7, last published 2025-01-13, `deprecated: null`, `recharts ^2.13.3` peer — and `tremorlabs/tremor-npm` last pushed 2025-01-13 while the Raw repo pushed 2025-10-10. Raw charts are one copied module: `import { SparkAreaChart } from "@/components/SparkChart"`, `npm i recharts`, local `chartUtils` + `cx`, default `h-12 w-28`, fixed palette `blue|emerald|violet|amber|gray|cyan|pink|lime|fuchsia`. The **29 KPI blocks** are the richest published stat-tile vocabulary in this landscape: stat + delta badge, stat + "from {previousStat}", arrow-icon delta, `ProgressCircle` ring at `radius={25} strokeWidth={5}`, `ProgressBar` with "used of limit", `CategoryBar` split (completion vs prompt tokens), `SparkAreaChart` inline in the tile, status pills `within`/`observe`/`critical` with "N/5 goals". **Tremor ships no agent surface**: `www.tremor.so/llms.txt` and `blocks.tremor.so/llms.txt` both 404, and no MCP server, skill or registry JSON is referenced on the docs pages read.

**21st.dev** renamed its tool: "Magic MCP is the former name of the 21st MCP. Same tool, renamed"; `@21st-dev/magic` persists as an alias proxying to `https://21st.dev/api/mcp` since v0.2.0, old keys were reset, and legacy tool names auto-translate (`21st_magic_component_builder`→`generate`, `logo_search`→`search_logo`). Install is `npx @21st-dev/cli@latest init --client <cursor|claude|codex|vscode|devin>`. Tools: `search` (free, metadata), `get_component` (retrieval, paid), `get_inspiration` (reranked against a project's Design Context), `search_logo`, `generate`. Catalogue is "12,000+ React components", React + Tailwind + TypeScript, shadcn-CLI installable, with a Charts category. Free tier: **2 code retrievals/day**. **Figma Dev Mode MCP could not be verified** — no primary source was retrieved, so nothing about it is asserted here.

Portability verdict: the shadcn **registry protocol** (JSON item spec, namespaced registries, `files[]`+`target`) is mirrorable as a basalt registry shape, and the **workflow** (`info --json` → search → docs → add `--dry-run` → read and verify the added files) is directly liftable. The KPI **information architecture** (stat + delta + one micro-visual + status qualifier) is the best thing to steal from Tremor. Everything class-name, token-name or Recharts-shaped must be re-implemented: Mantine 9's CSS-modules + theme-token model has no equivalent of a registry `target`-into-`aliases.ui` rewrite, and TanStack Charts is not Recharts.

## Refactoring UI packs and the rest of the field

**Premise correction, verified against the publisher:** Refactoring UI's 50-chapter TOC has **no dashboard chapter and no table chapter**. "Don't overlook empty states" sits in Finishing Touches (p203), and "Table styles" appears only in the component-gallery list. Any pack claiming Refactoring-UI-derived dashboard or table rules is extrapolating.

The packs are small, single-author, eval-free. `s0xDk/refactoring-ui-skill` (642 stars, created and last pushed 2026-08-26, licence field NOASSERTION though the README says MIT) ships `SKILL.md` + `references/{systems,diagnose,techniques}.md` + `assets/tokens.css`: fixed scales (spacing `4 8 12 … 768`, type `12 … 72`, 8–10 greys, "Write colors as HSL, not hex"), a 7-step procedure (**grayscale first**, choose by elimination, "Shrink the canvas" to ~400px), and 9 hard rules with 4.5:1 / 3:1 splits. Its total dashboard content is one line — "Dense UIs (dashboards) are legitimate — but as a deliberate decision, not a default" — plus hard rule 7 (distinguish series by _contrast_, light→dark of one colour, not hue). No KPI anatomy, no encoding table, no screenshot, no rubric. `jaywilburn/refactoring-ui-skill` (65 stars, MIT) is a single SKILL.md and carries the **best dashboard material**: a "Dense (dashboards, admin panels, developer tools, financial terminals)" preset at "Compact type (13-14px body), tighter spacing (12-24px between sections)", table variants ("Right-aligned numbers for decimal alignment", "Monospace or tabular-nums font feature"), empty states, and a "Design Audit Checklist" that doubles as a rubric. The two packs **encode incompatible typography**: jaywilburn recommends "a modular scale… based on a ~1.25 ratio" while s0xDk explicitly forbids "a modular scale built from a ratio (4:5, 2:3, golden ratio)". `LovroPodobnik/refactoring-ui-skill` (58 stars, no licence) and a `refactoring-ui` skill inside `wondelai/skills` (2,445 stars, 50 skills, v1.8.0 2026-08-29) round out the tail.

Real adoption is elsewhere. An aggregator's install-sorted UI/UX chart (809 skills, "Sources checked Sep 6, 2026") reports frontend-design 996.9K installs, design-taste-frontend 624.7K, ui-ux-pro-max 399.4K, impeccable 338.9K, interface-design 29.7K, superdesign 10.8K — and **no refactoring-ui-named skill in the top 50**. Treat these as aggregator-reported, not registry-official. `superdesigndev/superdesign-skill` (826 stars, MIT) is CLI-and-account bound (`.superdesign/design-system.md`, a "pixel perfect replica", `iterate-design-draft --mode branch`, share a preview for human feedback) with no rubric and a single dashboard mention. The closest analogue to basalt's stance is `dammyjay93/interface-design`: explicitly "For interface design — dashboards, apps, tools, admin panels. Not for marketing sites", persisting decisions to `.interface-design/system.md`, with "Precision & Density" and "Data & Analysis" direction presets and a `design-review` command with an approval bar — structurally what basalt does with rules + lint guards inside one framework.

## Does any of it work? The evidence layer

**No published evaluation of a design skill or craft-scoring system prompt exists** anywhere in this landscape. Skill-level craft claims are currently unfalsifiable by public benchmark. The nearest proxies:

- **Design2Code** (arXiv 2403.03163): 484 real webpages plus an 80-case HARD set; CLIP similarity plus element matching. GPT-4o direct prompting scores Block 93.0 / Text 98.2 / Position 85.5 / Color 84.1 / CLIP 90.4; on HARD Block-Match collapses to 56.6 and models "fail to generate 30%–40% of the block elements". Humans judged 64% of AI pages _better designed_ than the reference, yet **Fleiss' κ is only 0.46** for pairwise comparison — design labels are moderately reliable at best. Its rubric ordering is directly reusable: raters were told to prioritise **content > layout > style**. And a logistic regression shows _text_ similarity has a **negative** coefficient (−0.3541, p=0.021) — literal text match predicts losing.
- **WebDev Arena** (10 Mar 2025, updated 13 Jun 2025): Bradley-Terry over 80,000+ votes on generated Next.js/React apps, Claude 3.7 Sonnet #1 at a 76% average win rate; 26% ties and **18% "both bad"** votes, mostly from dependency/compile failures. Its ablation matters for skill authors: unconstrained generation beat JSON-schema-constrained output by +12.98 to +88.76 Arena points — harness scaffolding materially changes measured quality.
- **VisualWebArena** (arXiv 2401.13649): 910 execution-graded tasks; best VLM agent 16.37% vs **88.70% human** (230-task sample), GPT-4o+SoM later 19.78%, text-only GPT-4 7.25%. This is the only yardstick for _operational_ usability, and the gap is enormous.
- **Judge bias is measured, not hypothetical.** Arena's self-preference study (1,460 battles, 1 Jun–23 Sep 2026, 12 judges, 34,580 verdicts): models picked their own answer **58% vs humans' 34%**; AI judges agreed with other AIs 79.4% but with humans 56.9%; in 393 unanimous-AI battles humans agreed only 60.6%; humans called tie/both-bad 32% while one model forced a winner 96%. Any reviewer subagent that also authored the work is measuring its own taste.

Do repository context files help? The controlled literature is **null-to-negative and disagrees with itself**: arXiv 2602.11988 abstracts that context files "do not generally improve task success rates, while increasing inference cost by over 20% on average" and that "repository overviews, although popular and recommended by model providers, are not helpful"; arXiv 2607.27250 finds "Context strategy does not measurably move correctness on either agent (bounded to <=10-15pp via equivalence testing)" across 288 runs and attributes failures to "implementation skill… not missing repository knowledge"; arXiv 2606.20512 reports the opposite direction on SWE-bench Verified — 33.0% mean resolve vs 28.3% static and 25.5% unguided — but attributes the gain to **coverage** (+14.5pp evaluable patches) with per-patch precision flat at ~59%. Read together: context guidance helps an agent find the right file, not make better changes.

For dashboards specifically, IBM Carbon files Donut, Pie, Stacked bar, **Bullet, Meter and Gauge** under _part-to-whole_ and opens with "Start by identifying the purpose of the visualization and then choose the appropriate chart type"; NN/g's preattentive guidance says visualisations "should leverage human cognition and use length and 2D position to communicate quantitative information quickly". Those two pull in opposite directions on rings and gauges.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Micro-visuals in KPI tiles.** Keel's reference design (given) uses ring-for-rate, gauge-vs-target, progress-with-target-tick, tick distribution and segmented budget. Tremor ships 29 KPI blocks doing the same. `/dataviz` blesses the stat tile, a same-ramp "meter" and a 12-point sparkline. Impeccable's craft floor bans "sparklines, progress rings, and soft-shadowed rounded rectangles standing in for content" and the "hero-metric template". NN/g's length-and-position argument is the strongest _technical_ case against the **ring**: angle encodings are read worse than length. Adjudication for basalt: keep the four micro-visuals, but require each to encode a **target or limit** (which ring-for-rate and gauge-vs-target do and a bare sparkline does not), and prefer the linear/ramp forms where a target exists.
2. **Type scale.** Four positions: jaywilburn's 1.25 modular ratio; s0xDk's explicit ban on ratio scales; ui-ux-pro-max's fixed `12 14 16 18 24 32`; impeccable's "fixed rem scale, not fluid" with a tight 1.125–1.2 step. For dense UI the two fixed-scale positions agree and match Refactoring UI's own spirit; the 1.25 ratio produces steps far too coarse for 13px dashboards.
3. **Accessibility at design time vs audit time.** Vercel makes a11y MUST/NEVER rules; impeccable deliberately quarantines it because "models over-cautious themselves into safe, underdesigned output". Basalt's reviewer already scores craft, expression and communication separately, which is closer to impeccable's model.
4. **Charts' status in the hierarchy.** ui-ux-pro-max ranks data viz last with "split into multiple charts"; `/dataviz`, Tremor and Carbon all treat data viz as first-class. Do not inherit ui-ux-pro-max's priority table.
5. **Refactoring UI provenance.** The book has no dashboard or table chapter; the packs disagree with each other on the fundamentals while both claiming the book as source.

## What `basalt-design` should adopt, ranked

1. **Compute the palette; never eyeball it.** Port the six checks as a `basalt-ui init/sync`-shipped validator: OKLCH L band ~0.43–0.77 light, chroma floor ≥0.10, CVD ΔE ≥ 8 target / ≥6 only with secondary encoding, **normal-vision worst-pair ΔE ≥ 15 as a non-negotiable gate**, ≥3:1 vs surface, fixed categorical order that never cycles (a 9th series folds into "Other"). This is the single highest-value, most enforceable artifact in the landscape and it fits basalt's existing guard model better than any prose rule.
2. **A stat-tile contract with named slots.** `label` (sentence case, no colon) · `value` (semibold, auto-compact `1,284 / 12.9K / $4.2M`) · optional `delta` (signed, "vs <named period>", colour = direction × whether up is good) · optional 12-point trend sparkline in a de-emphasis hue with the current period in accent. This formalises Keel's "KPI tile with a micro-visual per dataset" into something a reviewer subagent can score slot by slot.
3. **A "is it even a chart?" router ahead of the encoding table.** Single current value → stat tile, not a one-bar chart; a handful of headline numbers → KPI row, not a grouped bar; one ratio against a limit → meter with a same-ramp track, not a two-slice pie; >~7 meaningful classes → a table, not more colours; <4 data points → stat card, not a line; 3–10 target-vs-actual metrics → a bullet-chart grid. This slots directly into basalt's existing scenario → encoding-catalogue step.
4. **Nine non-negotiables, stated as checkable sentences.** One axis, never dual-axis; colour follows the entity never its rank; categorical in fixed order, never cycled; sequential one hue light→dark; diverging two hues with a neutral grey midpoint; never a rainbow; status colours reserved, always icon + label, never colour alone; text wears text tokens, never the series colour; markers ≥8px and hit areas ≥24px.
5. **Number typography split.** Compact axis ticks from 1,000 up (`0 / 1K / 2K`); **proportional figures for hero/display numbers, `tabular-nums` only in columns and axis ticks**. This is the precise correction to the common blanket `tabular-nums` rule, and it is backed independently by Vercel's MUST and by Tremor's table styling.
6. **Tooltips enhance, never gate.** Every value reachable without hover (direct labels or the table-view toggle); same content on keyboard focus; crosshair on lines, mark-as-hit-target on bars; one tooltip for all series; "values lead, labels follow"; label text via `textContent`, never `innerHTML`. Vercel has no popover guidance at all and no table-view escape hatch — this is where basalt can be strictly better than the best-adopted checklist.
7. **Dashboard filter grammar and "refetch keeps the frame".** One left-aligned filter row above the content it scopes, date range first, filters scope everything below, never per-chart; on reload hold the previous render at reduced opacity — no skeleton, no layout jump. Concrete, missing from every other pack, and directly relevant to a TanStack Query app.
8. **A deterministic rule detector wired as an edit hook.** Copy impeccable's architecture, not its rules: an LLM-free detector set, a before-edit hook that can block a bad write, and exit codes `0` clean / `2` findings / `1` scan failure. basalt's lint guards already exist; this gives them a stable machine contract and a rule count you can publish and version.
9. **Two isolated reviewer passes with an honesty banner.** Keep `basalt-design-reviewer` as Assessment A (design/craft), add a second, isolated Assessment B that can only see the render plus a deterministic detector — and if the review runs inline, require a `DEGRADED` banner as the first line. Given Arena's self-preference numbers (58% vs 34%), a single agent scoring its own output is the weakest possible configuration.
10. **A scored rubric with renormalisation and a closing contract.** Nielsen's 10 heuristics at 0–4 with a total that renormalises when heuristics are n/a, P0–P3 severities each with a suggested fix, a snapshot persisted per run with a 5-run trend, and a mandatory closing question or a literal `Questions skipped: <reason>`. Add Design2Code's ordering — judge **content first, layout second, style third** — to the reviewer prompt so cited crops argue about the right thing.
11. **Batched screenshot rounds, not per-tweak trips.** Keep the ≥3 rounds but make each round a single batched look at the render ("run them together in each batched look at the render, not as separate screenshot trips"), checking contrast, shadow, spacing rhythm, measure, states and browser-surface details (selection, caret, scrollbars, focus rings, and "the numerals in tabular data") in one pass.
12. **Code-led over comp-led.** Adopt impeccable's measured preference: comps of working screens scored 2.0–2.9 while code-led builds of the same briefs scored 3–3.5. For basalt this argues for screenshotting the real Mantine render via `basalt-ui shot` early, rather than mocking in HTML.
13. **Router-plus-references structure.** Every mature artifact here converges on the same shape: a small SKILL.md that routes, with detail in on-demand references (Anthropic's own docs: "a skill's body loads only when it's used"; ui-ux-pro-max 16KB on the load path against ~3MB of data; `/dataviz` SKILL.md as a router over seven references). Keep scenario → concept → encoding catalogue in the router; push per-encoding detail, number formatting, mark specs and the rubric into references.
14. **Name the density instead of defaulting to it.** ui-ux-pro-max's `--density` dial (high = "Dense/dashboard (8-32px spacing scale)") and jaywilburn's Dense preset ("13-14px body, 12-24px between sections") both show the value of stating density as an explicit, chosen preset. Basalt is dense by default; making that a named, documented mode turns an accident into a defended decision.
15. **An anti-defaults section that names a _dashboard_ tell.** Frontend-design's five AI tells are the best-known example of this genre. Basalt's version should be specific to its domain: identical equal-width rounded cards in a grid, one border-radius regardless of hierarchy, the same soft grey shadow under every tile, and a delta badge with no stated comparison period.
16. **States and dead ends as MUSTs.** Vercel's "Design empty/sparse/dense/error states" and "No dead ends" are the two cheapest rules to enforce and the most often skipped in generated dashboards.
17. **Table craft.** Right-align numeric columns with tabular figures, sticky header, zebra or hover row treatment at dense settings, and `aria-sort` on sortable headers. Note this is assembled from Refactoring-UI and ui-ux-pro-max rows, not from Vercel, which has no table guidance.

## What to reject

1. **Impeccable's blanket bans on the hero-metric tile, sparklines/rings/progress rectangles and "identical card grids."** They are aimed at marketing and AI-slop surfaces and directly contradict Keel's KPI grammar and composite 2×2 cards. Take the _test_ (does this micro-visual carry a job?), not the prohibition.
2. **Boldness machinery: `bolder` / `delight` / `overdrive` / `colorize`, plus the `concept-seed` roll with re-roll registers and a decision page.** A random direction assignment is the opposite of a design system with a fixed token space, and basalt already deliberately overrides "bold aesthetic" pushes.
3. **Any randomisation or "seven visual systems" derivation step.** Same reason; basalt's constraint is a product, not a portfolio.
4. **`ui-ux-pro-max`'s priority table**, which ranks Charts & Data #10/10 at "LOW" impact — invert it for a dashboard framework.
5. **`data-density`'s "split into multiple charts if needed"** as a blanket rule. Cross-series comparison in one frame is the whole point of an operational dashboard; the constraints that actually protect legibility are encoding discipline, a table fallback, and target-anchored micro-visuals — not fragmentation.
6. **Landing-page layout guidance**: `max-w-6xl / 7xl` container widths, hero-centric section templates, and a 375/768/1024/1440 breakpoint set as the whole story. Dashboards need ≥1440 and fluid grid behaviour.
7. **The Rust engine / downloaded-binary runtime model.** Keep `basalt-ui` a Node CLI; the impeccable project has already had to add mechanical version gates ("the two drifted to 4.1.0 against 4.5.1 in 2026-10 and npm kept serving an engine seven releases old") — a dependency surface basalt does not need.
8. **Tailwind/Next vocabulary in rules.** `focus-visible:ring-*`, `min-w-0`, `line-clamp-*`, the Next image `priority` prop, and the entire Hydration section. Translate to `:focus-visible` CSS and Mantine theme tokens, or drop.
9. **MUST/NEVER-only severity with no priorities.** Vercel's three words are the only severity vocabulary in the project, and `command.md` carries no rule IDs and no severity at all. Keep P0–P3.
10. **Unversioned, unfalsifiable rules** ("always use the latest stable version") and **hand-maintained content counts**. ui-ux-pro-max's own manifests disagree with its data (84/98 vs 79/119); generate counts mechanically or don't publish them.
11. **Installing shadcn/Tremor/21st.dev code.** Every block is Tailwind classes, Radix APIs, `text-tremor-*` tokens and Recharts prop shapes. Harvest the _information architecture_ and the registry protocol shape; do not adopt the dependencies.
12. **Refactoring UI's "shrink the canvas to ~400px" and grayscale-first as mandatory gates**, and **HSL-not-hex as dogma.** Both are single-column, marketing-shaped procedures; a dashboard's first constraint is a multi-column grid under real data.
13. **Single-agent self-scoring.** Given measured self-preference (58% vs 34%), a reviewer pass that shares context with the builder must be labelled degraded, not trusted.
14. **Catalogue matching as a design method.** Paralect's "most generic page" verdict and Skillselion's "no argument about why a page should look one way rather than another" are the failure mode of the whole `--design-system` genre.
