# Charts

How a chart earns its place and its marks. Props are in the generated docs: [CartesianChart](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/cartesian-chart.md), [ChartCard](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/chart-card.md); the law is `basalt-charts.md`.

## Pick the form

- Start from the question's row in the AGENTS block table; a chart is the answer only when the reader compares or follows a shape. One value, fewer than four points or a ratio against a limit is a [StatCard](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/stat-card.md) or a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md).
- A ranked list or a split of a total is a [BarList](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/bar-list.md): one shared scale, the value printed, the legend left out.
- More than about seven classes to tell apart is a [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md).
- A part-to-whole of a few named parts is a one-row stacked [BarList](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/bar-list.md) (`series` names the parts, the row's `parts` carries their values) or, for one value on named zones, a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md), where a bar of hand-sized `div`s draws none of that ([part to whole](#part-to-whole)).
- Two weekday-by-hour (or service-by-day) grids on one scale are [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md)s that share one focus ([heatmap tools](#heatmap-tools)).
- Many near-identical series (six hourly lines in one 0 to 15 % band, the same grey pile on four cards) answer "which leads" through the table only. Draw the leader in the accent against a quiet rest in ink steps and name it at its end, or give each row a `c.spark` ([table](table.md)) beside its figure; six lines in one band are noise.

## Part to whole

- A count of a whole draws its share: a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) or a row `bar` (a [StatCell](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/stat-cell.md) `meter`, a `rows` entry's `bar`) beside the figure, where the figure alone or a footer row that restates it leaves the whole unmarked ("Finals in all 3,800" as a footer row; "Enriched 612 of 900" with a running-total sparkline and the 900 undrawn).

- The row takes its parts' colours from the app's series file, in the order `series` lists them, so the bar, its legend rows and every other list of the same parts show one colour per part. A second blue for the same part (`BASALT.brand` on the bar and the series blue in the list) breaks "swatches match the marks".
- The parts are named where the eye is: the part that answers carries its value on its segment (`chartLabels.fits` places it where it fits), so segments are read by name where a legend would be read by order.
- The legend rows join the page's focus both ways: spread `focus.item(i)` (rows of the page's items) or `focus.seriesItem(key)` (rows that stand for a series) on each, so a focused segment lights its row and a focused row lights its segment, and the figure's period follows through `focus.readout`. Two near greys for a dimmed part and a focused one read as equal: take the dim from the kit alone.
- Worked, how much of the year is tagged: a first build drew the tags as `div` segments, listed them in a legend that joined neither focus nor series, spent a second blue on the untagged segment and left its 38 % in the legend. The fix spread `tagFocus.item(i)` on the rows, used one blue and labelled the segments. Draw it as one stacked [BarList](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/bar-list.md) row from the start: the colours, the focus and the labels are then the kit's.

## Colour budget

- Neutral by default, one accent, one status hue. The accent marks the one series or mark class the question is about, counted: a second series in the accent needs a second meaning the page names in a word, and otherwise goes to an ink step (one page drew both its series in one blue; the owner: "choose colour only where expression needs it"). One measure drawn at two grains is two classes: a heat ramp and the detail zone of the same usable hours, or a running total and its daily bars, each in the accent, count twice, so the second grain takes an ink step. A second series in the accent, across two cards included, needs the word on the surface: what blue means is in the legend or the card's context line, the design file alone is too little (a page drew two pools in one blue and named the meaning in `DESIGN.md`).
- A part of a whole or a category split is read from position, order and a direct label. A hue per part is a rainbow.
- A ramp starts neutral below its threshold and turns hue only past it; zero stays neutral.
- A state has one colour in every linked view: the legend swatches and a table's status dots match the marks; a rise is one hue in the chart and in the table pill, and a fall the other; the accent marks the answer (the next run, the leader) and nothing else, so one word keeps one colour across parts (one page drew a rise in the chart and a fall in the table).
- The accent is the pointed-at mark and the rest of its series stays in ink: a 30-day score line in the accent where the question points at today is the whole series in the accent; draw the line in ink and today's dot in the accent.
- On a page of figures a delta pill takes its tint from its polarity (`up-good`, `up-bad`) where the direction is good or bad for the reader, and is `polarity: 'neutral'` where it is neither; a warn tone or dot beside a red and green pair of pills is a second hue, so the third fact takes a neutral dot or a word (one page drew amber beside a red `up-bad` pill, another an amber dot beside a green and red pair). Two accent lines told apart by `dash` alone take a name at each end through `chartLabels`, or a legend entry.
- A status hue means a status: carry it with a word or a dot, not hue alone. [lint:status-fill-as-text]
- The accent goes to the item that answers (the culprit hop, the signal that leads the verdict), chosen from the data, so it follows the culprit whichever series that is: one build fixed it to the gateway, so with the home fixture the culprit router was grey; another spent it on a 6 px difficulty dot while the progress bars carried none. One fact takes one hue: a red delta pill beside an amber 'Behind pace' dot said one fact twice, and a Down figure drawn without a hue beside table dots with one split a state in two.
- A status hue marks a state that needs action; a target rule, a "live" band and a passing delta stay in ink steps, and the failed mark takes the status fill instead of its category hue.
- The common state stays neutral and the state that needs action takes the hue: one page put amber "Waiting" on 16 of 25 rows, so the one row that needed a hand stayed hidden among them; colour the few.
- Kinds told apart by colour take the series file's pairs, and an HTML mark (a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) bar, a track `div`, a dot) reads them as CSS variables: `groupTokens('<group>', tokens.series.<group>)` from `basalt-ui/tokens` gives `var(--basalt-<group>-<key>)` refs for the pairs of `src/basalt.tokens.ts`, so a kind keeps one colour in the chart, the list and the track. Kinds drawn as close greys or one grey dot fail: "Pass", "Never run", "Low" and "Medium" on one grey dot, Focus told from Personal by close greys (`groupTokens` over the series file is the one route to colour HTML marks), kind carried by the accent and two grey steps read off a legend. The one status hue goes to the state that needs action (a stuck or overdue row) and nothing else shares it.
- A quiet or zero day reads as calm, and its delta stays neutral: a delta pill turned red on an empty Saturday ("−3.1 vs 3.1 a day"). A delta against an empty or partial period stays neutral, and two kinds one grey step apart (Fix, Chore) take distinct ink steps or a direct label.
- **One meaning per colour across cards.** Write `colour: meaning` for every hue in the 1800 frame; a hue with two meanings, or one fact in two hues, is cut. One page's blue meant both 'today' and 'the list that grows', with a green pill beside the red; another's Atlas Large blue meant 'tried first' in the chart and 'served' in the list. Give the second meaning an ink step or a word, and keep the status hue and the series hue apart. The cards of one view share the hue count, and a table's status column is one of the cards: one page painted its leader and its priciest project in two accent bars, and the State column showed amber Quiet beside red Cost spike, three meanings for two hues; another did the same with one blue. Write the `colour: meaning` list over the cards and the table columns together, one status hue for the one state that needs action, and every other state in an ink step or a word.

## One measure

- The hero, the marks and the baseline measure one quantity. One hero was minutes down after a reboot while its dots were total minutes down and its baseline stood against nothing: three quantities. Pick the quantity the question names, then derive the hero, the marks and the baseline from it.
- A question that compares a group against its control draws both groups on one axis and leads with the signed difference ("-2 min vs held back"); drops after a reboot against their control read on one axis, and a date scatter hides the comparison.
- A target for an unfinished period is prorated, or its pill stays neutral ([copy](copy.md)).

## A headline value has a baseline

- Give every headline figure a baseline: a zero line, a reference line, a zone, or a signed delta that names its period. `refLines` and `zones` are data on [CartesianChart](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/cartesian-chart.md). [type:ChartRefLine] [type:ChartZone]
- Against a limit, draw the limit and print the distance to it; "steady at 1.6x" without the 3x line is a claim without a scale. A limit that exists only as a colour rule becomes geometry or a signed distance.

- The threshold that defines the answer is drawn on the chart, where a footer value alone leaves the reader a claim without a scale: where "part" means 4 kt apart, a `refLines` rule or a zone at 4 kt shows it, and the mark that matters carries why it matters ("Models part: over 4 kt apart"), beyond its name.

## Label and annotate the mark that matters

- Label marks directly with `text` marks placed by [chartLabels](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/chart-labels.md): it measures, avoids overlaps and drops what is too wide. The worked example is region `example:chartLabels` in `apps/playground/src/recipes/seam-examples.tsx`.
- Annotate the one mark the page is about (the best run, the onset, the live stage) and let the rest recede. [useChartFocus](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/use-chart-focus.md) holds one focused datum for the marks, the card fact and the live region; region `example:useChartFocus` in the same file.
- The hero states the answer with its verb ("Runs out 13:52", where "13:52" alone answers nothing) and at most one phrase of baseline; the one mark the page is about carries the annotation at rest, so the sentences around it say something else ([copy](copy.md)). A list keeps the answer above its cut.
- When the answer needs a word on one mark (the slowest, the spike, last night), compose [CartesianChart](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/cartesian-chart.md) with a `text` mark placed by `chartLabels`: a kind such as [Bars](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/bars.md) takes only its own marks. Engine `text` marks from `@tanstack/charts/text` are allowed in custom marks. On focus the figure's period and status carry what the mark means (`focus.readout` takes any value: [linked parts](#linked-parts)) (its share, its model, its stage) while the headline stays the answer, and return on leave. An answer that is a sentence, or a readout that is a bare total, is the defect.
- Name the few marks that matter. On a band axis `x.ticks` as a list of keys labels exactly those bands (`ticks: ['Acme', 'Globex']`); a number only thins. Default thinning is for an ordered axis (time, a number): a band axis of names left to it showed 6 of 14 detectors, and a numeric tick that nothing on the page explains (25,600 on a settings axis) is the same gap. List the keys the answer needs, the answering bar first, and check the shot names each. Elsewhere an engine `text` mark placed by `chartLabels`. A band or zone that is the answer carries its words on the mark, where a faint fill with an empty face leaves the reader to guess. A rule at 25, 50 and 75 is a zone with a `label` where a bare number would stand, and a dense series (480 samples over one day) is binned to the grain the question reads, where the raw line is a scribble.
- The mark that answers carries the answer at rest, placed by `chartLabels`: the leading line's end value, the total on each top bar, the gap at today. Worked: a hero said "−3 vs pace" and nothing on the chart marked today or the gap to the pace line; an RHR line that exits the band was left unmarked; a bar chart printed a value on none of its bars. The hero and the annotation state it once between them.
- The answer sits on the mark that answers, as its value or its name, with the hero or a footer as a second place at most. Cases: the untagged share on its 38 % segment, the production sha on the "Deployed" rule's label, the top blocker's row and bar, the slid measures named in the headline and the 38 h spike labelled, the mover's figure beside its name. A label the plot edge drops is placed again inside the plot. A hero, a `sub` line or a sentence beside the mark is the second place, and the label on the mark stays: the leading source's name sits on its own row or cell, the verdict word ('held', 'blocked') on each row that is held, the longest wait as a value on its row or bar, the decision hour as a label on that hour.
- Name each line at its own end with a `text` mark placed by `chartLabels` (the series name, and the end value where it differs), so the legend is the second place: a usual line and a due step, or a harbour and an offshore line, named in the legend only are the defect.
- A state a clause of the question asks for ("waiting") is a word on its item, through the bar series' `values: (v, d) => text` callback, where a second pane of bars carries its only signal as two zero ticks (the two waiting events drew the same as the rest in the net pane).
- On pages that count where a mark could name: the rows that answer carry a verdict at rest, the word on the row (one page counted 'yours, stuck' while !433 and !441 sat as bare rows and the 3-week !391 stood unannotated); the failing items are named, where a strip that counts failing probes and names none leaves the reader to scan the table; a window or a tide that stays under the bar is annotated on its span (a neap tide under the bar went unremarked, its go bands were without a label or key, and 'bar dry' misread 2.4 m); the at-risk tickets are marked on the chart. The answer stands on the mark, and a count is its second place.
- A fact the footer states that belongs to one mark is on that mark: a `highlight` or per-bar `opacity` for a state, `values` for a number, a `refLines` rule on every pane for a median, a legend-only entry for a fill without a series. Cases: ask-again detectors counted in the footer while the highlight covered only `d.past`; a median stated as 'Median 7,372 steps' with a rule on the minutes pane and none on the steps pane; three guessed prices drawn like measured bars with the caveat in the footer (`opacity` plus a legend entry). The read-aloud then keeps the mark as the slot and cuts or rewords the footer. A count of named things names them where they fit ('3 of 14' beside the three detector names), and the footer keeps the count only where the names fail to fit.
- Agreement and extremes at rest: when sources agree or part, the agreement is on the mark (one pill said '+3.1 °C GFS' and the 2-of-3 counts lived in the table, and Fri night, where ECMWF alone dips under 0 °C for 3 h, went unmarked); an extreme item is drawn from every source, where the median alone hides it (frost zones came from the median, so a one-model frost drew nothing and its meter read 0); the items that cross the line are marked on the dots where a chip only counts them (three tasks over a month late, with labels placed by `chartLabels.place`).
- Name every line and every rule at its end: a dotted reference without a label and a sparkline without an end value fail (`reference={lead.costUsd}` unlabelled, Pressure and Baseline named only by the legend; [Sparkline](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/sparkline.md) takes `axisLabels`, `BarListItem.secondary` can carry a share). A hero line that must name its rule (the week crossing, the limit, the oldest point) is a [CartesianChart](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/cartesian-chart.md) with a `refLines` rule that carries its label and a `text` mark on the point; a [Sparkline](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/sparkline.md) `reference` draws a bare line and its `xLabels` strip sits off the line, and a sparkline that restates the table's meter column is cut.
- Custom marks read `plot.rows(key)` and `plot.visible`, so a legend toggle hides the mark, and a custom `text` mark sets `fontSize` and `fill` from tokens (`BASALT.text.micro`, `BASALT.ink`): without them it draws about 15 px in the body ink ([custom marks](../../basalt-charts/references/custom-marks.md)). [type:ChartPlot]

## One name

- An item has one name across the axis, the list and the hero. One axis said "Food", "Trips", "Power" while the list and the hero said the full category names. A name that is too long is cut by the kit's own truncation; an invented short one splits the item in two.

## One readout

- When the figure's period follows focus, the tooltip is off (`tooltip={false}`) and the period, the dimmed marks and the list row are the one readout. A tooltip, a swapped hero and a lit row are three readouts for one pointer.
- The announcement names the focused item's values in the tooltip's order (`name: a, b, c`), and a verdict alone is too little: one announcement said "stable" with the values left out.
- Charts share one cursor by default, so two cards whose readouts differ each take their own `cursorGroup`; a Backlog card drew a guide while its readout stayed at the total, and a cursor group per chart fixed it.

## Anatomy of a chart card

- The headline figure, its unit, delta, period and status live in the card's `figure`; a card action lives in the card's own menu or controls, not in the page bar. [type:ChartCardProps]
- The status is a dot and its word ([Status](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/status.md)), in the figure. [lint:status-fill-as-text]
- The footer holds at most two facts. [type:FootFacts]
- Region `chart-card` in `apps/playground/src/routes/overview.tsx` is the composed card; regions `weight-chart`, `domain-chart`, `shared-x` and `plot-marks` in `apps/playground/src/routes/charts.tsx` are the chart bodies.

## Linked parts

- The focus readout carries every measure the parts draw, in the figure's period or status or a strip cell's `sub`. One readout wrote the daily outside spend while pane one drew the rolling total, so the gateway key's rolling value and the distance to the cap showed nowhere; a bar card drew its numbers only in the live region and a table below the fold at 390. List the measures each part draws and check that the focus writes each one.
- The readout fits one line at 390 and carries every measure. `period` and `status` are each one line the kit cuts, so a readout written as one mono sentence ended '40 min moderate, …' at 390 and the day's steps stayed hidden. Write the readout as short value forms, one measure each ('7,372 steps', '40 min mod.'), the window and name in `period` and the measures in `status`, or split the measures across `period` and `status`; shot the 390 frame on a focused datum and read both lines. A readout that names only the busiest state while the four peaks sit in the live region is the same defect: the readout carries every value the announcement does.
- A part at a grain a focus arm cannot reach is folded into a part that joins, or dropped; "no list at the day grain" in DESIGN.md is a part left standing. Two bars, Practice and Reviews, held items the Items table held, without `focusItems.holds` and without a mark on the table's two past-due rows.

- A card sparkline drawn on the chart's x either joins its focus or is folded into the chart: a [Sparkline](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/sparkline.md) draws its own points and leaves the chart's focus untouched, so two sparklines that show the chart's days and sit apart from its focus fail, and a flat 120 px sparkline with a `reference` 110 px off the line is a pill's claim without geometry. Where a chart and a list, a table or a timeline show the same items, they share one focus: pointing at or stepping to an item in either focuses it in the other, both ways, and the rest dims in both.
- The figure's value and delta are the answer and never follow focus (`basalt/primitive-bypass` refuses `focus.readout` there: a headline swapped and its card resized on every hour); the focused reading goes in `period` and `status` through `focus.readout(rest, read)`, which takes any value (one page put a placeholder 'partial' delta on its figure). The focused period and status carry the focused item's own number, so a figure that stays on the whole period never sits beside a period tag that names one day or week: 78 beside 'Push day 23 Oct', '20 ended' beside W41. Write the readout as 'Push day 23 Oct, 85' (name and number), keep the figure as the answer, and keep the status to one line on a span focus. [lint:primitive-bypass]
- One [useChartFocus](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/use-chart-focus.md) is passed to the chart host as `focus` and spread on each row of the list through `focus.item(i)`; the list sits in its own [ScrollArea](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/scroll-area.md) at the chart's height, and the keyboard step scrolls the focused row into view. The wiring is the snippet in the basalt-charts skill.
- Stacked panes on one x each take the one `focus` (`<Lines focus={focus}>` on every pane); a top pane alone leaves the others out of the focus.
- A chart of a parent's children (56 idle days under eight weeks) joins the parent's focus through `focus.hold(indexes)`, as `focusItems.holds` does for a list: one cursor lights the week and its days, where days on a cursor of their own are a second focus.
- A list of groups (versions, releases) beside samples of those groups spreads `focus.groupItem(name)` on each row instead; the recipe is `seam-linked` in `docs/recipes/`.
- A page without a chart still has one focus. Hand-drawn rows (a pair list, a bespoke meter row) spread `focus.target` on their container and `focus.item(i)` on each row, and a [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md) takes `focus`; the arrow keys step the rows and `focus.readout` writes the focused row into the figure's period or status. Four pair rows drawn with none left the first tab stop on the info button and the arrows announced nothing; a table tinted a row and dimmed nothing. A page of tables, with nothing to chart, still takes one `useChartFocus` over the rows it shows. Each [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md) gets it as `focus` (`getRowId` maps a row to its item), a focused row dims the rest, and the figure's period or the stat cell reads the focused item through `focus.readout`. Pages built from tables and stat cells that wired a hover tint only (the shipped rule asks for focus 'chart or none') tinted one row, and nothing dimmed or read out.
- Where a chart and a list could show different grains, draw the chart at the list's grain: one bar per ticket, grouped by status zones, so a table row and a bar are the same item and `DataTable focus` joins them by id. Bars per status beside a table of tickets joined nothing; one bar per ticket joined them.
- A part at a grain that none of the focus arms reaches is folded into a part that joins, or dropped. A per-account fact beside a grid of cells is a [BarList](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/bar-list.md) on `focusItems` (one bar per account, the cell's reading as its value: one page wrote 'Fri 14:00' as text in three places); a daily chart of a table's children beside a table that takes one focus arm is joined (`focus.hold`) or left out, since a chart without `focus` leaves the focused wallet's line unmarked (and [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md) takes one of `focus`, `focusSeries` and `focusItems`).
- The kit owns the dim and the clear: the page takes the kit's dim for a seam-drawn mark, and rows that take focus sit under a container whose pointer leave clears (`focus.target`), so the gap between two rows keeps the last focus.
- A row that stands for a span holds it by window: `focusItems={{ focus, holds: (row, item) => item.at >= row.from && item.at <= row.to }}` lights the row for every item inside it, where an equality on the minute lit a row only when the focused minute was the event's own (the hour before an event held nothing).

## Form from structure

- An ordinal (a difficulty, a level, a rank) is a mark, where a word in a column reads rather than sees: one page printed '4 Hard'; five pips or a segmented [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) draw it.
- The items the question names go on the chart's axis: the stalled books on the time axis, linked to their rows, the hops in path order, where a table beside a chart of something else leaves the question to the reader.
- Where the items carry a structure the question asks about (hops in a graph, two scores per item, the stages of a pipeline, the stages of a night), the form draws that structure: the hops as a path, the pair as a scatter or paired marks, the stages as segments. A ranked list or a total-only stack is the fallback, and the page names it as the fallback. Spans over time (revisions with a sent time and a wait, outages, a hold) are an [IntervalTrack](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/interval-track.md) in place of a table with a meter; and every measure the question names is drawn, so a period or a wind speed the question asks about is a mark or a second panel on the shared axis, with the table as the list beside it.

## One view

- A table that answers the question sits above the fold at 1800x1200; at y 966 only four rows show; the cost told in a meter, a bill card and a spend card is one part too many. Where two charts share an axis and answer the two halves of one question, they are one chart above and below the axis (a diverging chart) in one card, where two cards and a strip would stack (as would a strip, per-day bars, a list and a line, four stacked parts). Charts on one x share one card, or sit side by side inside the 1800x1200 fold; the second below the fold is the defect. Two cards stand only when the axes differ. A list longer than the chart sits beside it in its own scroll region at `@wide`, and a box takes its content's height, so a fixed height cannot leave a gap or clip. A chart is as tall as its marks need: one build drew eight dots in a 490 px chart, others short charts across 750 and 550 px boxes. A card is sized to what it holds: one page drew its direction scatter in a second card whose hour axis ran narrower than the wind chart's (ticks at x 1105 against 336), where one card with one axis lines the hours up; another's hero card was 750 by 256 px for one number and a flat line while its table reserved 14 rows for 17 of 30 Issues. Run the check on your 1800 shot and derive a row count from the data or the fold.

- Cards in one row end at one height: a short card is given its content's height or a second fact, so the row ends flush (row-1 cards ending at 597 and 647 px; a legend leaving 140 px empty to match its list; cards 240 px each for a figure and a line).
- **Size each part to its content.** The box takes what it holds, so a fixed or stretched size past it is the defect: a [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md) of a few rows takes `height` (or `height` per container class), and `matchHeight` only inside a card its rows fill (five rows drew a 540 px card with 160 px cells); a forced `min-h` stays within the rows it holds (`@wide:min-h-160` left 160 px empty under eight rows); `bodySize={{ rows }}` sizes a table panel per layout, where a hand `height` leaves a band at 390; every cell of a strip takes footer `rows`, so one tall cell leaves the others without a blank band under them (a strip stood 226 px for one sparkline); and a tall card stands only beside a column of its own height (a bars card stretched to 862 px for twelve bars). Read the part heights in the one-view table before the shot: a part more than a third taller than its marks is resized and the page shot again.
- Peer cards that each answer one dimension of one question (four settings of one lens, each its own card, focus and footer) are joined into one answer: one figure above them that says the question's answer, or one line or shared axis that crosses them, where four equal cards leave the reader to add them up. A part that answers a dimension of the page's one question is a row of one card, where a card of its own splits the answer.
- Two parts that share a focus share one legend and one caption: two heatmaps on one scale carried two legends and two captions, and the lower half of the 1800 frame was empty. One legend sits above both, one caption under them; a chart and its day list are one card, the list scrolling in its own region with a cue where it is cut.
- A page of many small cards groups them by verdict and leads with the group that needs action (one page sorted slid first but nothing marked where the slid group ended, so the viewer scanned pill colours); on a phone the verdict reads above the fold (eight cards ran 2,000 px at 390 and two and a half showed above the tab bar), and every phone card keeps a label for its figure (one showed an Issue id over "Unblocks: 23").
- A part that restates another part's measures is folded into the chart's rows or dropped: a weeks table took about 530 px to say what its charts drew. A card large for six bars is cut to the bars' height (a Backlog card, two-thirds wide), and two cards on different axes with an empty lower half join one surface or lose one.
- Parts that answer one question sit beside each other in one view, inside the 1800x1200 fold at `@wide`. The defects seen: findings apart from their probes with half the page empty, a window table in a separate section below its chart, a flush table beside a card, two cards with two items below the fold.
- **A form laid out along time or along a chain takes the row's full width, its list below.** [IntervalTrack](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/interval-track.md), [ChainView](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/chain-view.md) and a timeline read along one axis, so every span or column is as wide as the card and half the card is half the reading. The list or table that shares its focus sits under it on the same `useChartFocus`; a half-width card beside a list is for a form that reads at half width (a bar list, a sparkline, a donut, a short chart). Put in the half cell, a week track beside its event table cut nine titles to "Sprint…" and "Desig…" at 1800, and a chain beside its table lost its third column at the panel edge. Both read right with the track or chain across the row and the table under it, so the "sit beside each other" rule above covers only the parts that read at half width.
- Three stacked bands that share one focus apiece are three pages in one: one page drew a strip, five bars 1,480 px wide and, below them, the table of the same monitors. Join the list to the bars through one focus and put them side by side at `@wide`; run the check on your own 1800 shot before the fact count.
- A part that restates another's rows or figures is folded into it or dropped, whatever its form: one page drew a heat grid and an in/out list beside the status table that held the same six rows, and another a Totals card of two 4-bar charts under a strip that printed its 31 and 5, as a bare card without anatomy. Ask of each part which figure or row only it says; a part with none is folded.
- A table that shows the chart's items is [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md) with `focus`, which keeps the link through its own sort; a list that only repeats the chart's numbers is folded into the chart card or dropped; a table of the chart's items joins its card instead of a third stacked surface.

- **The check is a table, judged by numbers.** Where the repo lets you (`docs/design/<screen>.md`, else a file beside the page source) write one row per part at 1800x1200: `part | question it answers | x,y w×h | what it joins`. The page fails on any of: two parts that answer one question in two rows; a part full width that holds four rows or one mark; a gap as wide as a card in the first 1200 px; a part that joins nothing and lacks a word for why it stays. The fix changes the surface (which parts share a card, a legend or a focus) and the shot is retaken; padding changes nothing here. It catches a card stacked on a full-width table of wide gaps, where at 390 a tap on a row changed a readout off screen; a Backlog and Done pair that made a second row joining nothing above; three stacked cards with the detail away from the mark it explains; three full-width cards, four event rows across 1500 px over about 90 px of dead body; four cards, a 240 px card for a 14-day sparkline and six columns across 1500 px; and two cards side by side that each carried their own figure and footer, so they read as two cards. A [DataTable](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/data-table.md) in a half-width cell (about 730 to 750 px) folds into phone cards unless its priority 1 and 2 columns sum to 480 px or less (`ch` × 8 + 24 per column, [table](table.md)), so a table stacked full width under the chart leaves a gap of 800 px between two columns. Add that sum to the part's row before you place a table beside its chart: when it passes 480, demote a column to priority 3 or give the table the wider column, and when every column must stay, name the stack as a kit gap in a marker. Side by side is one view when the two share a figure, a legend and a focus: the sparkline folds into its figure's card, the event rows sit beside the chart they explain, and a part that joins nothing is cut or says in a word why it stays.

## Encodings

- A share bar is scaled to the whole its caption names: "of 64 GB" is 64 GB, where the sum of the classes shown is a different whole.
- A list carries the chart's encoding of a state (the same fill, the same grey); a second encoding of one state reads as two states.
- A ranking prints its values on the bars; at 390 a band axis names every key through a short `x.ticks` label ("Owner", "CI"), where a number only thins the keys away.
- At 390 every item of a band axis is named: `x.tickFormat` writes the short tick apart from the readout (`x.format`), so nine event names fit where the full names let 6 of 9 through; on a [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md) `formatCol` returning `''` thins ticks and `tickCols` only adds them. Count the ticks in the 390 shot against the items.

## Progress against a target

- A [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) against a limit has zones and a scale that holds the data: `meter={{ max: limit, format }}` without `zones` pegged a 190 min hold full against a 60 min limit and showed nothing past it (`MeterSpec.zones`), and `min`/`max` of 0.5 x lo and 1.5 x hi drew 0.12 and 0.01 at one x and pinned 3.17 at the right end with nothing marking the clamp. Take `min`/`max` from the data's extremes with the zone edges inside them, so the value stays in its range; the meter itself clamps silently. Only the zone the value is in takes a status tint, the outer zones stay `neutral` (red and tan bands on every row, the Balanced one too).

- Progress against a target is a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) with zones (behind pace, on pace, ahead), or a bar of done against the pace on one measure; a rule belongs on bars that measure it. One build floated 'Pace: 78' over To do, In progress and In review, which hold points by status, where pace is another quantity; a [Meter](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/meter.md) with zones draws done against pace.
- The hero stands against the reference the data holds, said once in the hero or its pill: working hours for 'free' time (a '5 h free' stood against nothing), the approvals a merge needs for 'stuck', the 24 h default after which a review goes ahead on its own for 'held up' (a bare 28 and a 'Review ahead in 1 h' left its review unnamed).

## Honest marks

- Every fill a mark uses has a legend entry: a series, or a legend-only entry for a fill without a series of its own. A `highlight` fill is one: a blue `highlight` segment replaced the ink step of the leading target and lacked a swatch; a legend swatch grey beside bars in the brand blue (an Unrated entry) is the same defect. Pass `legend={...}` to [ChartCard](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/chart-card.md). [type:LegendEntry]
- A mark's state is named by what it means ('so far', 'estimate', 'rated') and its look stays out of the word ('lighter', 'dim'). Opacity reads darker on one scheme and lighter on the other ('Lighter bar' at 0.55 read dimmer in dark), and a latest bucket at half opacity and without a word leaves the reader to guess. The word goes in the legend-only entry beside the fill, or on the mark.
- A dot series whose dots coincide or touch (equal values, a heap near zero, 18 dots in a chain at a 100 % ceiling) sets `spread: 'x'` or `'y'` on the series, so every dot has its own place and a ring stays countable; the tooltip keeps the true value, and the axis that takes a few pixels without a misread is the one to spread ([cartesian-chart guide](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/cartesian-chart-guide.md)). One page drew 391 dots in a 150 px band with none, and its rings were one grey mass at 390. A `spread` series says it nudges (a unit caption: 'dots nudged apart'), and the axis ends where the data ends: one column stopped at 99 on an axis that ran to 100, so the last column hung off the last tick.
- An axis or a zone ends where its last slot ends: a zone built over `[first.at, last.at]` stops a slot short of a span that ends at its start plus the step, and leaves a white strip between two zones; and every tick label differs from the first, so a day axis that starts on 00:00 ends on another label (take `xTicks` to the last slot's start, or name the closing edge).
- Every series that prints a value sets `format` with its unit, so the tooltip and every readout carry it: a pool series printed a bare "0" while its siblings set `format`.
- A stepped or binned scale says its step in a caption; a size channel gets a key.
- A fixed row or bar count (`rows={8}`) is derived from the data, so the extreme fixture shows every item: hop 9 hidden behind eight rows, with a headline saying nine hops, is the defect (the bars summed to 18 of 19).
- An incomplete datum (a partial day, a night missing a stage) is marked on its mark (hatched, hollow or a caption on the bar) and named once; a complete smaller value would misstate it (a night with REM missing read as a finished 305 min; today's partial bar read as a finished day; a zero or absent day drew neither a mark nor a cue).
- The fixture shows its zero and its extreme at rest, so the card is judged with an empty hour and an over-limit value on screen. The extreme reads true: rounding or clamping it away hides it. A zero fixture shows a cue (a dash, a baseline) where the marks would be.
- One value that crushes the rest is capped with `y.domain`: the kit draws the off-scale caret and the tooltip keeps the true value (a 420 ms value rescaled the axis and flattened every other hop; eleven bars shrank to slivers). A ranking that saturates says how many are hidden (five identical 1,440 min bars with fifteen more cut), and a zero series gets a visible mark: a finished line at zero lay on the baseline rule and read as absent.
- An extreme fixture keeps the rest of the page readable, at 1800 and at 390. A [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md) caps its colour `domain` below the extreme (values past it take the top step) and `annotate`s the clipped cell with its true value; a mark per over-limit hour thins to a zone or a run with one label on its peak once the marks would merge into a bead (a flood flattened every other cell to one colour; a storm drew 48 red dots over the line they sat on).
- The answer is a label on its mark and an outlier is capped, where a hero alone leaves a plot with its upper 40 % empty: one page left the single 14.6 dot unlabelled and unscaled for the other steps (`text` mark by `chartLabels`, `y.domain` clamp). Keep your own scale over the data: a cap you write yourself (a domain max below the largest value, a `Math.min` on the value) draws that bar at the cap and says nothing; a y cap at 10 drew a 15 bar at 10. Either the scale holds the data, or the cap is the kit's `y.domain` clamp, which draws the off-scale caret and keeps the true value in the tooltip.
- Binned bars carry a unit caption ("per 15 min"). A count axis and a share-of-limit axis carry a caption (`y.title`: "Requests", "% of limit"), and a `y.format` keeps a decimal for a domain under 2 units (ticks `0.5`, `1.0`, `1.5`, where `1`, `1`, `2` repeats a label).

## Heatmap tools

- At 390 a heat grid omits its cell values, so a value that answers needs another carrier: a 190 batch and its two 22 cells lost their labels, and the footer's "US$186" stood unexplained beside the cell's "190". Name the cell in the footer ("Wed 03:00") or put the value in a list under the grid.

- A [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md) shows magnitude by hue, so the cells that answer carry their value: `annotate` returns a short mark for a datum (a two-digit hour, a peak glyph) and `null` for the rest, and the few cells that matter are the few it marks.
- The legend says the cap and the unit: `formatLegend` ends the ramp at its cap ('4+') where the colour domain is capped, `legend.label` names the unit, and `tooltip.valueLabel` names the measure where the default says 'Value' (one legend drew '0 ... 4' without a cap or a unit, another '0 / 123' and a 'Value' tooltip). A cell wider than its mark takes a compact `annotate` ('1.2k', '4+'); the kit drops a note wider than its cell, so a row of bare grey is an `annotate` too wide for its cell.
- A cell that is partial (a bucket that missed some of its samples) is a `states` entry returned by `getState`, drawn and legended as its own state; without it an hour that missed one to three of its buckets looks complete.
- `tickCols` keeps the column ticks the reader tells time by: the kit thins ticks to 00, 05, 10, which fall on odd hours; list the stops that matter ("00", "06", "12", "18") and they survive. `tickCols` only adds ticks to the kit's stride, so a column that must go is thinned by `formatCol` returning `''` for it (one grid drew -8, -6, -4, -2, -1 and Next where four were asked).
- A [Heatmap](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/heatmap.md) takes `getRow`, `getCol`, `getValue`, `getState`, `tickCols` and its `data` with a stable identity (module scope or memoized): in a build without the React Compiler a focused grid re-renders in a loop (React error 185 on hover; the doc names it).
- Worked, when the line is busiest: cells that answered ("Busiest Thu 20:15") showed their value only by hue (`annotate`), a partial hour looked complete (`states`) and a bare "0" on Mon 04:00 stood without a key beside hour ticks at 00, 05, 10 (`tickCols`). Two grids on one scale also share one legend and one caption ([one view](#one-view)).

## A time window picks its resolution

- Up to 7 days the bucket is the hour, unless the data itself is daily; beyond that the day. Work that arrives in bursts through the day reads wrong by the day: a daily bar over 7 days hid that most of the work landed in a few hours (software-factory Console, Flow and Improve: 24 h and 7 d by the hour, 30 d by the day).
- A bar series denser than about one bar per 3 px at the plot's width is drawn as an area (`mark: 'line'` with `area: 'fade'`) or as merged buckets: 168 hourly bars in 342 px at 390 alternated 1 and 2 px and read as a hash. Keep the hour for the 24 h window and merge to the day, or draw the week as an area, at 390.
- The bucket is named on the chart: a unit caption ("per hour", "per day") and x labels for the window's start, now and end.
- A bucket takes the busiest sample (max) for a load-like series (queue depth, CPU, concurrency), so a spike survives the binning (a mean averages it away), and the sum for a count.
- One window control per page (24 h, 7 d, 30 d, opening on 7 d), and every chart on the page follows it. A chart with its own window beside another is two questions on one page.
- A sliding window's query key is quantised onto the data's step with [windowKey](node_modules/basalt-ui/docs/window-key.md), where the step is the bucket.

## Finish of a form the page draws

- A focused item gets its own mark beside the container's ring, and the ring is inset so it clears the text inside.
- Figures in the page's own markup take tabular numerals; every transition is motion-safe; every value is on the ladder.
- A status token is a fill or a border in a CSS module, and text takes a text token, because the lint stops short of a CSS module.
